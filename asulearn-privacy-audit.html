ASULearn: a privacy & data transparency audit

What does App State openly tell students about their data? This audit compares what's publicly disclosed against what AsULearn's own integrations actually do, benchmarked against peer universities running the same platforms.

Executive Summary

Why audit a university's LMS privacy practices?

Every App State student uses AsULearn (soon to be canvas) to access coursework and submit their assignments. There's no opt-out for this. That makes one thing non-negotiable: students should be able to easily access information regarding what the platform collects, who sees it, and how long it sticks around. This audit tests whether that's actually true, using nothing but public sources: no login access, no special requests, nothing a student couldn't find themselves.

The short version: App State's institutional privacy practices are real and reasonably thorough, but they're not built for a modern LMS. The university's main privacy statement predates several of the tools AsULearn now runs on top of, and no dedicated notice explains what those integrations actually do with student data.

A privacy policy that's technically accurate but practically undiscoverable isn't really functioning as a privacy policy.
Methodology

How the audit was scoped

This is not a technical audit. It doesn't involve intercepting network traffic or reverse-engineering the platform. Instead, it works intuitively, using open source intelligence, the way a student actually would... starting from App State's public privacy statement, following every embedded link, and checking whether AsULearn's own help documentation, app listings, and support pages match what that statement claims.

Three source types were reviewed: (1) Appalachian State's institutional Privacy Statement and GDPR notice, both published on appstate.edu; (2) AsULearn/Moodle support documentation published by App State's IT knowledge base and the AsULearn mobile app's own listing; and (3) dedicated LMS privacy notices published by peer universities using the same Canvas or Moodle platforms, used as a benchmark for what "thorough" looks like.

What the university's privacy statement actually covers

App State's Privacy Statement, last revised February 2020, is mostly scoped to appstate.edu and the network of sites the university owns. It's a pretty solid, general-purpose policy, as it discloses the use of Google Analytics and cookies, explains data-sharing limits with third-party vendors, and includes a full GDPR rights section for international students. It also explicitly invites individual departments to publish more specific privacy statements for their own platforms if/when needed.

AsULearn is exactly the kind of platform that invitation seems written for; and it doesn't have one. There's no AsULearn- or Canvas-specific privacy notice describing what the LMS collects beyond what's implied by the general statement.

What AsULearn's documentation reveals

App State's IT kbase describes AsULearn as a Moodle 4-based LMS that integrates with Zoom, Kaltura, VoiceThread, Pearson, and Google Assignments, among other tools. Each of those integrations is a separate data relationship; video attendance data through Zoom, engagement analytics through Kaltura, third-party assignment data through Pearson; and none of them are named or explained in the university's general privacy statement. The support documentation itself only states that AsULearn "ensures student privacy within the course," without defining what that means in practice.

Findings

Where the gaps are

No LMS-specific privacy notice. Peer institutions on the same Canvas platform; including UBC and Simon Fraser University; publish dedicated Canvas privacy pages explaining exactly what server logs, IP addresses, and usage data the LMS collects. App State currently relies on its general, five-year-old website privacy statement to cover this instead.

Third-party integrations aren't disclosed as data relationships. Tools like Zoom, Kaltura, and Pearson are listed as features in support docs, not flagged as places where student data leaves the LMS. A student reading only the privacy statement would have no way to know these integrations exist.

The policy predates the platform it now needs to cover. The 2020 revision date sits before AsULearn's current integration list and well before the 2026 migration to Canvas; a transition significant enough to change what data is collected and how, without a corresponding privacy update found in this audit.

Learning analytics go unmentioned. Modern LMS platforms typically log activity completion, time-on-task, and engagement patterns for analytics purposes. Published research on Canvas at other institutions confirms this kind of data is collected by default; App State's public documentation doesn't confirm or deny whether the same applies here.

Discoverability is the core problem, not intent. Nothing found in this audit suggests bad-faith data practices; the issue is that accurate information is scattered across a general privacy statement, a GDPR notice, and disconnected support articles, with no single page a student could point to and say "this is what AsULearn does with my data."

The GDPR notice sets a standard the rest of the site doesn't match. App State does publish a detailed GDPR privacy notice for EEA-based visitors, proving the institution is capable of platform-specific disclosure when required to. That same level of detail hasn't been extended to U.S. students using AsULearn day to day.

Recommendations

Closing the gap

01

Publish a dedicated LMS privacy notice

Give AsULearn/Canvas its own privacy page; modeled on the GDPR notice App State already maintains; covering what's logged, retained, and shared through the platform itself.

02

Disclose third-party integrations by name

List Zoom, Kaltura, Pearson, VoiceThread, and Google Assignments explicitly as data-sharing relationships, not just as course features, with a one-line summary of what each tool can view.

03

Tie privacy updates to platform migrations

Treat the 2026 Canvas migration as a trigger for a privacy review; new platforms mean new data flows, and the policy should move when the technology does.

04

Clarify learning-analytics use in plain language

State directly whether engagement and completion data is collected for analytics, and if so, who can access it and what it's used for; instructors, administrators, or both.

05

Put the answer one click from the login page

Link the LMS-specific notice directly from the AsULearn and Canvas login pages, so the information is discoverable at the exact moment a student might look for it.

06

Match the GDPR notice's level of detail

Use the university's own GDPR notice as the internal bar; if that level of specificity is achievable for international visitors, it's achievable for the LMS every student is required to use.
