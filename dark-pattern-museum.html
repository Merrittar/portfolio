Curator's Note

Why catalog manipulation?

A dark pattern is an interface built on purpose to trick you into an action you didn't intend: agreeing to something, paying for something, giving up something you'd have declined if the choice had been presented honestly. None of these are bugs. They're deliberate decisions that trade your trust for a short-term metric.

This museum doesn't call out any specific company; every exhibit below is an original mockup built to demonstrate the mechanism, not a screenshot of a real product. The goal is recognition: once you've seen the pattern named and isolated, it's much harder to miss in the wild.

The Collection

Six exhibits

Exhibit 01

Confirmshaming

Guilt-tripping the decline option so strongly that saying no feels like admitting something embarrassing.

Yes, sign me up for savings No thanks, I hate saving money
Exhibit 02

Roach Motel

Easy to get in, deliberately hard to get out; signup is one click, cancellation is a maze.

Start free trial →
Cancel anytime: Settings → Account → Billing → Manage Plan → Downgrade → Confirm → Call support
Exhibit 03

Sneak Into Basket

An extra item; travel insurance, a warranty, a "recommended" add-on; silently pre-checked before checkout.

Add Priority Support; $4.99/mo
Add Extended Warranty; $12.99
Priority Support was added automatically based on your cart.
Exhibit 04

Hidden Costs

The price looks final until the very last screen, where fees quietly appear one at a time.

Subtotal$42.00
Service fee$6.50
Convenience fee$3.25
Total$51.75
Fees revealed only after entering payment details.
Exhibit 05

Trick Questions

Double negatives and inverted logic in consent language, designed so the "correct" box is easy to mischeck.

Don't not subscribe me to updates
Exhibit 06

Forced Continuity

A free trial that silently converts into a paid subscription, with the billing notice buried in fine print.

Your trial ends today; card on file will be charged
Notice shown once, in a dismissible banner, on day 14 of 14.
Design Principles

The opposite of a dark pattern

01

Symmetry of effort

If signing up takes one click, canceling should take roughly one click too. Asymmetric friction is the tell.

02

Default to opt-in, not opt-out

Anything with a cost; money, data, or a recurring commitment; should require an active, unambiguous choice.

03

Price the whole thing up front

Every fee that will eventually appear should be visible at the point where the user is actually deciding.
